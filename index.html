Problem Statement & Context

The explosive dissemination of COVID-19 across 2020 presented unprecedented challenges to epidemiological surveillance and resource planning. This project utilizes the authoritative time-series repository published by Johns Hopkins University Center for Systems Science and Engineering (JHU CSSE) to model dynamic transmission rates, evaluate regression architectures, and simulate forward healthcare requirements.

Dataset & Modeling Specifications

  • Time Span: Jan 22, 2020 – Sep 21, 2020 (244 continuous daily observations)
  • Geographic Scope: 188 sovereign countries and territories
  • Split Scheme: Sequential 80/20 TimeSeriesSplit without lookahead leakage
  • Primary Target: Cumulative Confirmed & Daily New Incident Cases

Scientific Limitations & Model Boundaries

Synthesis of Key Findings

The project demonstrates that non-linear gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost) combined with auto-regressive lag signals (Lag-1, Lag-7) and rolling window aggregations achieve superior forecasting fidelity (R² > 0.99) compared to naive linear regressions during steep outbreak acceleration stages. Hyperparameter tuning via RandomizedSearchCV reduced test prediction error by over 50%.

Recommended Future Extensions

  • • SEIR Hybrid Integration: Coupling machine learning gradient boosting with biological compartmental differential equations to enforce herd-immunity saturation.
  • • Exogenous Policy Signals: Incorporating Oxford Stringency Index and Apple/Google Mobility indices as dynamic time-varying covariates.
  • • Sub-National District Modeling: Scaling the pipeline to localized administrative levels to resolve micro-cluster hotspots.
